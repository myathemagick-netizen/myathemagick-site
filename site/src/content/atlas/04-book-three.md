modified Heisenberg uncertainty relation: Δ(information)·Δ(time) \~ ℏ³U,
where U is Carroll's Ubiquity constant (\~6×10⁠⁠, the ratio of the
universe's scale to the Planck scale). The result is approximately 10⁷
bits per second --- the maximum rate of information indeterminacy
quantum uncertainty permits. Worth noting: the word thaum itself is
borrowed from Terry Pratchett's Discworld series, where it is the
standard unit of magical output (defined in the canon as the magic
needed to create one small white pigeon or three billiard balls).
Carroll's contribution was giving the borrowed term a real quantitative
derivation, not independently arriving at the same concept. The whole
book uses a "Roundworld vs. Discworld" framing device explicitly
throughout. Any resemblance between the thaum and the chronon is
therefore at minimum one step removed from a meaningful convergence and
should be treated as such.

Carroll died unexpectedly on April 22, 2026, at 72. The Octavo (2010)
and a 2022 interview collection, Interview with a Wizard, represent his
final major theoretical statements. He left the tradition with its most
mathematically rigorous framework and its most explicit account of why
the probability field operates the way it does. What he could not supply
--- and what the system attempts to provide --- is the mechanism beneath
the mechanism: why the chronon field has tidal dynamics, why riftlines
concentrate chronon density, why the gnosis state specifically increases
chronon permeability rather than simply producing a pleasant altered
condition. The Octavo points at the right territory. The MMS attempts to
map it.
