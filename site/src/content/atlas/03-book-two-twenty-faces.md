## **The Twenty Faces --- The Icosahedral Plates**

The icosahedron's 20 triangular faces are the plates of the planetary
chronon geometry --- each face bounded by three riftline corridors
meeting at three Earth Pillar nodes, each face a region of the planetary
field with its own internal circulation dynamics. Where the 30 edges
(riftlines) are the zones of elevated chronon activity and the 12
vertices (nodes) are the points of maximum concentration, the faces are
the relatively stable interior regions between them. Chronon activity is
lowest at the face centers and highest at the edges. The faces are named
here primarily by geographic region, with mythological designations
where geography gives insufficient guidance --- particularly for the
largely oceanic southern and Pacific faces.

Each face is defined by its three bounding edges and three corner nodes.
The naming follows the dominant geographic feature or mythological
tradition of the region enclosed. The 20 faces distribute as follows: 5
northern polar faces (each sharing the North Pole node), 5 southern
polar faces (each sharing the South Pole node), and 10 equatorial faces
arranged in an alternating band around the planet's middle.

### **Northern Polar Faces --- The Arctic Crown**

Each northern polar face has the North Pole node as one vertex and two
adjacent northern tier nodes as the other two, bounded by two polar
edges and one circumpolar edge.

### **Face 1: The Greenland Face**

Bounded by: NP-BT (Sargasso Corridor), NP-AM (Hyperborean Corridor),
BT-AM (Atlantean Seam). Nodes: NP, BT, AM. Geographic center: Greenland
and the North Atlantic. The interior of this face encompasses Greenland
--- the world's largest island, 80% ice-covered, containing the greatest
volume of frozen freshwater outside Antarctica. The Norse settlement of
Greenland, the Thule Inuit tradition, and the Norse mythological
geography of the frozen north all encode the character of this chronon
plate. The face is bounded by the most geomagnetically significant
corridors in the northern mapping: the Sargasso's Temagami anomaly to
the west, the Hyperborean's Kursk anomaly to the east, and the Atlantean
Seam's LLSVP connection beneath.

### **Face 2: The Eurasian Steppe Face**

Bounded by: NP-AM (Hyperborean Corridor), NP-MD (Shamanic Spine), AM-MD
(Cradle Road). Nodes: NP, AM, MD. Geographic center: the Eurasian
steppe, from Scandinavia through Russia and Central Asia. The largest
contiguous land mass on Earth enclosed within a single icosahedral face.
The Eurasian steppe cultures --- Scythians, Sarmatians, Huns, Turks,
Mongols --- all arose within this plate, all showing the
chronon-permeable qualities of cultures living between three active
riftline corridors simultaneously. The Mongol Empire's extraordinary
territorial reach followed this face's edges precisely.

### **Face 3: The Siberian Face**

Bounded by: NP-MD (Shamanic Spine), NP-DS (Dragon's Spine), MD-DS
(Himalayan Bridge). Nodes: NP, MD, DS. Geographic center: Siberia and
the Russian Far East. The world's largest permafrost zone, the Tunguska
impact site (1908 --- a chronon field disruption event at the Shamanic
Spine's heart node), the Lake Baikal basin (the world's deepest lake,
containing 20% of Earth's unfrozen fresh surface water, sitting in a
geological rift). The Shamanic Spine runs directly through this face's
western edge and the Dragon's Spine along its eastern edge --- the most
heavily shamanic icosahedral plate in the northern tier.

### **Face 4: The North Pacific Face**

Bounded by: NP-DS (Dragon's Spine), NP-HI (Raven Road), DS-HI (Pacific
River). Nodes: NP, DS, HI. Geographic center: the North Pacific Ocean.
Largely oceanic. The North Pacific gyre circulates within this face, its
clockwise rotation expressing the dextrogyric face circulation of the
northern plate. The Aleutian island chain --- one of the most
volcanically active arcs on Earth --- runs along the face's interior.
The Beringia land bridge that once crossed this face was the primary
human migration route into the Americas. The Ainu people of Hokkaido and
the Aleut peoples of Alaska are the indigenous cultures of this face's
margins.

### **Face 5: The Canadian Arctic Face**

Bounded by: NP-HI (Raven Road), NP-BT (Sargasso Corridor), HI-BT
(Turtle's Back). Nodes: NP, HI, BT. Geographic center: the Canadian
Arctic, Hudson Bay, and the Great Lakes. The interior of this face
encompasses Hudson Bay --- an inland sea formed by glacial depression,
whose tidal dynamics express the face's mesogyric circulation. The Great
Lakes system sits at the face's southern reach. The Haudenosaunee
Confederacy --- the Iroquois League, one of the most sophisticated
indigenous political systems in North America --- arose within this
plate. The Turtle Island tradition that names the North American
continent as the back of a great turtle is the mythological encoding of
this face's geography: the face is shaped like a turtle's back, its
edges rising toward the North Pole node at the apex.

### **Southern Polar Faces --- The Antarctic Foundation**

Each southern polar face has the South Pole node as one vertex and two
adjacent southern tier nodes as the other two, bounded by two polar
edges and one southern circumpolar edge.

### **Face 6: The Weddell Face**

Bounded by: SP-SAA (Antarctic Transmission), SP-ZM (Bone Road), SAA-ZM
(South Atlantic Anomaly Line). Nodes: SP, SAA, ZM. Geographic center:
the Weddell Sea and Antarctica's Atlantic sector. The Weddell Sea ---
the most iceberg-dense ocean on Earth, home to the Weddell Seal and the
Antarctic krill fishery that underpins the southern food web. The
corridor between the most geomagnetically anomalous seam on Earth
(SAA-ZM) and the south polar anchor: the face most directly associated
with the Belialian catastrophe's electromagnetic residue.

### **Face 7: The Ross Face**

Bounded by: SP-ZM (Bone Road), SP-WB (Southern Deep), ZM-WB (Kerguelen
Line). Nodes: SP, ZM, WB. Geographic center: Antarctica's Indian Ocean
sector and the southern Indian Ocean. The Ross Ice Shelf ---
Antarctica's largest ice shelf, the size of France --- occupies the
western portion of this face. The Kerguelen Plateau's submerged
microcontinent sits along the Kerguelen Line at the face's northern
edge.

### **Face 8: The Southern Ocean Face**

Bounded by: SP-WB (Southern Deep), SP-LI (Songline Root), WB-LI
(Songline Spine). Nodes: SP, WB, LI. Geographic center: the Southern
Ocean south of Australia. The deepest reach of the songline network's
chronon geometry: the face whose northern edge is the Songline Spine
(Edge 18) and whose southern corner is the South Pole. The Antarctic
Circumpolar Current --- the world's most powerful ocean current, the
only one to flow entirely around the globe unobstructed --- runs along
the southern margins of this face. The most levogyric ocean circulation
on Earth, completing a full planetary circumnavigation driven by the
counter-rotation of the southern polar plate against the equatorial
belt.

### **Face 9: The South Pacific Polar Face**

Bounded by: SP-LI (Songline Root), SP-EI (Navel Cord), LI-EI (Polynesian
Highway). Nodes: SP, LI, EI. Geographic center: the South Pacific polar
region. Te Pito o te Henua (Easter Island) is the northern apex of this
face's Navel Cord corridor. The face interior is the least navigated and
least studied region of the Pacific --- the southern polar face of the
Polynesian plate, the deep water south of the navigational network.
Point Nemo, the oceanic pole of inaccessibility, sits at the center of
the adjacent equatorial face to the north; the chronon desert of the
face center is the predicted complement to the richly maintained
riftline at the face's northern edge.

### **Face 10: The Drake Face**

Bounded by: SP-EI (Navel Cord), SP-SAA (Antarctic Transmission), EI-SAA
(Patagonian Gate). Nodes: SP, EI, SAA. Geographic center: the Drake
Passage. The most turbulent ocean crossing on Earth, where the Atlantic
and Pacific meet at the Drake Passage with nothing to interrupt the
circumpolar current. The face interior is the convergence zone of two
polar corridors and the Patagonian Gate. The Tierra del Fuego
archipelago sits at the face's northern reach --- the southernmost
inhabited land on Earth, home to the Yaghan people who survived in one
of the most extreme climates on the planet without clothing, their
bodies generating sufficient heat through sustained mesogyric metabolic
activity.

### **Equatorial Faces --- The Living Belt**

The ten equatorial faces form an alternating band around the planet's
middle where the northern and southern chronon flows intersect. These
are the most culturally dense faces in the mapping --- the band of
maximum differential motion, maximum mesogyric activity, and maximum
civilizational concentration. The equatorial faces alternate between
those pointing north (with a northern node at apex) and those pointing
south (with a southern node at apex).

### **Face 11: The Atlantic Face**

Bounded by: BT-AM (Atlantean Seam), BT-SAA (Middle Passage), AM-SAA
(Trade Wind Road). Nodes: BT, AM, SAA. Geographic center: the central
North and South Atlantic. The entire Atlantic Ocean's cultural geography
within one icosahedral face: the Azores at the Atlantean Seam's
midpoint, the Caribbean at the Middle Passage's top, the West African
coast where the Trade Wind Road meets land. The transatlantic slave
trade's triangular route maps precisely onto this face's three edges.
The syncretic traditions born from that crossing --- Vodou, Candomblé,
Santeria --- are this face's most precisely documented riftline
maintenance cultures.

### **Face 12: The African Face**

Bounded by: AM-MD (Cradle Road), AM-ZM (Great Rift), MD-ZM (Dhow Road).
Nodes: AM, MD, ZM. Geographic center: the African continent. The most
historically significant equatorial face: all three bounding edges are
among the most culturally saturated corridors in the mapping. The entire
African continent from North Africa to southern Africa, from the Sahara
to the Congo Basin, sits within this face. The Great Rift Valley runs
along the western edge; the Cradle Road runs along the northern edge;
the Dhow Road runs along the eastern edge. Three of the most significant
riftline corridors in the planetary mapping enclose the continent that
produced Homo sapiens. Africa is not the cradle of civilization by
accident.

### **Face 13: The Indian Ocean Face**

Bounded by: MD-DS (Himalayan Bridge), MD-WB (Monsoon Bridge), DS-WB
(Wallace Line). Nodes: MD, DS, WB. Geographic center: the Indian Ocean.
The monsoon system that made the Indian Ocean crossable in both
directions runs within this face, its seasonal reversal expressing the
face's mesogyric oscillation. Indonesia --- the world's largest
archipelago and most biodiverse marine region --- sits at the Wallace
Line's eastern edge. The Borobudur Buddhist temple in Java, the
Hindu-Buddhist civilization of Angkor Wat in Cambodia --- the great
maritime civilizations of Southeast Asia cluster within this face's
boundaries.

### **Face 14: The Western Pacific Face**

Bounded by: DS-HI (Pacific River), DS-LI (Stick Chart Road), HI-LI
(Polynesian Triangle's Western Arm). Nodes: DS, HI, LI. Geographic
center: the western central Pacific, Micronesia and the Marshall
Islands. The Marshall Islands stick chart navigation tradition --- wave
piloting as chronon field reading --- arose within this face.
Micronesia's extraordinary navigational cultures maintained the
riftlines of this face's three edges through the most technically
precise somatic chronon perception in the historical record.

### **Face 15: The Eastern Pacific Face**

Bounded by: HI-BT (Turtle's Back), HI-EI (Long Voyage), BT-EI (Drake
Corridor). Nodes: HI, BT, EI. Geographic center: the eastern Pacific.
The center of this face is the region of greatest oceanic emptiness in
the Pacific --- the chronon desert at the face's interior, surrounded by
some of the most navigated riftline edges in the Polynesian network. The
Mayan and Aztec civilizations of Mesoamerica sit at the face's
northeastern corner where the Bermuda Triangle node anchors the Turtle's
Back. The Quetzalcoatl tradition of the Mesoamerican riftline corridor
runs along this face's northern edge.

### **Face 16: The Amazonian Face**

Bounded by: BT-SAA (Middle Passage), BT-EI (Drake Corridor), SAA-EI
(Patagonian Gate). Nodes: BT, SAA, EI. Geographic center: South America.
The Amazon basin --- Earth's largest tropical rainforest, the planet's
primary oxygen production system, home to the greatest biodiversity on
land --- occupies the interior of this face. The ayahuasca traditions of
the Amazon are the most continuously maintained entheogenic riftline
access practices in the Americas, concentrated precisely within this
face's interior. The Inca civilization of the Andes ran along this
face's western margin. The face interior is one of the
highest-biodiversity chronon field regions on Earth.

### **Face 17: The Sub-Saharan Face**

Bounded by: AM-SAA (Trade Wind Road), AM-ZM (Great Rift), SAA-ZM (South
Atlantic Anomaly Line). Nodes: AM, SAA, ZM. Geographic center:
sub-Saharan West and Central Africa. The Dogon at the Bandiagara
Escarpment sit near the northern edge. The Congo Basin --- the world's
second-largest tropical rainforest, the planet's second lung ---
occupies the face's interior. The face is bounded on two sides by the
most geomagnetically anomalous corridors in the African mapping: the
Trade Wind Road to the north and the South Atlantic Anomaly Line to the
south. The Bantu expansion --- the most extensive human migration in
African prehistory, spreading Bantu languages and agricultural practices
across the entire continent south of the Sahara --- originated within
this face and spread outward along its bounding riftlines.

### **Face 18: The Maritime Southeast Asia Face**

Bounded by: MD-ZM (Dhow Road), MD-WB (Monsoon Bridge), ZM-WB (Kerguelen
Line). Nodes: MD, ZM, WB. Geographic center: the southern Indian Ocean
and Madagascar. Madagascar --- the world's fourth-largest island, an
extraordinary biodiversity hotspot whose fauna is 90% endemic, separated
from mainland Africa by the Mozambique Channel --- sits near this face's
western edge. The island's Austronesian-origin population (ancestors who
crossed the Indian Ocean from Southeast Asia) adds a Pacific cultural
thread to an African geographic location, a precise reflection of the
face's position between Indian Ocean corridors. The Swahili Coast's
ancient trading civilization runs along the Dhow Road at this face's
northern edge.

### **Face 19: The Australasian Face**

Bounded by: LI-WB (Songline Spine), WB-ZM (Kerguelen Line), LI-EI
(Polynesian Highway). Nodes: LI, WB, EI. Geographic center: Australia
and the Tasman Sea. The entire Australian continent sits within this
face, bounded on two sides by the songline network's primary corridors.
This is the face with the longest continuous human maintenance tradition
on Earth: 65,000 years of songline practice along the Songline Spine
(northern edge) and 65,000 years of the Songline Root's polar connection
through the adjacent southern face. Australia is not one riftline
corridor's responsibility. It is an entire icosahedral face whose every
edge has been maintained for longer than any other human tradition on
the planet.

### **Face 20: The Central Pacific Face**

Bounded by: EI-LI (Polynesian Highway), DS-LI (Stick Chart Road), DS-HI
(Pacific River). Wait --- this face is actually bounded by EI-LI, HI-EI,
and HI-LI, giving it nodes EI, HI, LI. Geographic center: the central
South Pacific. The face whose center is closest to Point Nemo --- the
oceanic pole of inaccessibility at 48°52'S, 123°23'W, the point on Earth
farthest from any land. This is the chronon desert: the face interior
farthest from all three bounding riftlines, the region of minimum
chronon activity in the Pacific, surrounded by the most actively
maintained Polynesian navigation corridors. The vast emptiness of the
central South Pacific is the predicted consequence of icosahedral face
geometry: maximum distance from all three edges means minimum chronon
concentration. Nothing lives there not because the ocean is
inhospitable, but because the field is quiet.
