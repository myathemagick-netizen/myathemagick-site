# Hollow Manor: a pointier house

From the author's references (a Queen Anne house dressed for Halloween, a
stone manor on a terrace, Phantom Manor, a chateau by night): steep roofs,
things that stick out and jut up, a skyline of points.

- **Roofs** go two up for every one in (they were one for one), and every
  two bays have their own peak, so a wing's roof is a row of points with
  valleys between, not one long hip. Tower spires go three up for one in.
- **Along the tops**: iron cresting on about half the ridge cells, a finial
  or weather vane on some; chimneys run higher.
- **Gargoyles** (`gargoyle`, a new prop that can go anywhere) crouch on the
  towers' corners, the porch and the gateposts.
- **Porch**: a stone floor outside the front door, two columns, its own
  steep roof and finial, steps down to the drive.
- **Bays and oriels**: about a quarter of the outside wall bays push their
  windows out a block (an oriel upstairs, on a corbel), nearly half of
  them lit.
- **On the grounds**: an iron arch over the gate; statues round the
  forecourt; a **carriage house** west of the drive (stone, a steep gable,
  a wide doorway, hay and a broken workbench inside).
- **Cobwebs** (`cobweb`, a block you walk through and brush away): up in
  the corners of the manor's rooms and halls, and in the cellar. Not yet
  in the rest of the world's ruins.
- **Rooms**: an organ room (`pipe_organ`, two wide and three high: "Play")
  among the rooms a manor may have; the dining room has a long table
  (`dining_table_long`).
- Each manor still comes out different (plan, heights, which rooms, where
  the bays and peaks fall).
