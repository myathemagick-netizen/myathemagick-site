# The estate's trees; the wood's canopy; graveyards out in the wood

- **The old tree**: on the lawn right of the drive (and often a second on
  the left): three across at the foot, the trunk leaning one way then
  another as it climbs 15-18 blocks, bare limbs going off at angles with a
  little leaf at their ends, roots heaved out of the grass. From the gate
  it stands in front of the house.
- **The avenue**: trees down both sides of the drive, six apart, their
  heads meeting over it; one in five is dead. Clumps of three in the
  corners of the park.
- **The wood's canopy closes**: Hollow Wood trees have heads seven across
  (five before), flatter, so neighbours join and the sky is mostly shut
  out under them.
- **Graveyards are out in the wood**: `Estates.yard` puts one in most wood
  cells (23 by 27 m, rows of stones, the crypt and its coffin, a lantern),
  beside a road where one passes, never within 150 m of a house. Finding
  one puts "Graveyard" on the compass and in the journal.
- **By the house**: a small family plot (a few stones in an iron fence) in
  place of the big graveyard.
